## Why a probe was needed

`payroll_summaries` and `staffing_thresholds` hold zero rows, and the single
`employee_payment_settings` row belongs to a `super_admin` who could already read it.
A permitted read of an empty table returns zero rows, which is indistinguishable from
a refused read. The probe inserts fixtures inside a transaction it rolls back, then
reads them while impersonating four signed-in users, so "the policy permits" is
measured separately from "the table has data".

Impersonation is `set local role authenticated` plus `set local request.jwt.claims`.
The role switch matters: `relforcerowsecurity` is false on these tables, so the owning
role bypasses every policy and a probe run as the owner would read everything.

## Result

28 probes, 4 actors. Before the migration, 8 failed — every one of them a read
Requirement 21, criteria 13 and 14 exist to restore. After, all 28 pass.

| Actor | Own summary | Other employee's summary | Summaries unfiltered | Own pay settings | Thresholds read | Thresholds UPDATE | `payroll_periods` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| employee A | 0 → **1** | n/a (is A) | 0 → **1** of 2 | 0 → **1** | 0 → **1** | 0 | 0 |
| employee B | 0 → **1** | **0** throughout | 0 → **1** of 2 | 0 (none exists) | 0 → **1** | 0 | 0 |
| manager | 0 (none exists) | **0** throughout | 0 | 0 (none exists) | 0 → **1** | 0 | 0 |
| super_admin | n/a (none exists) | 1 throughout | 2 throughout | 2 throughout | 1 throughout | 1 throughout | 1 throughout |

Reading the columns:

- **Own rows readable.** Both employees reach their own summary and A reaches A's pay
  settings. This is the criterion 13 restoration.
- **Another employee's rows stay hidden.** B never sees A's summary, before or after.
  The policy is scoped by `profile_id = auth.uid()`, so widening the read did not widen
  the scope.
- **Unfiltered read returns own rows only.** Two summaries exist; each employee sees
  one. An employee cannot enumerate the payroll table by omitting a filter.
- **Every signed-in user reads the thresholds.** All four actors, including the
  non-administrator manager. This is the criterion 14 restoration.
- **No write was granted.** The new policies are `for select`; a non-administrator's
  UPDATE on a threshold affects zero rows. Only `super_admin` writes.
- **No administrator capability moved.** The `super_admin` column is unchanged across
  every probe, and the manager gains nothing beyond the own-row read every employee
  gains. Pay rates, payroll settings, clock-entry corrections, time-off approvals, and
  work schedules remain `super_admin`-exclusive (Requirement 21, criterion 12).
- **`payroll_periods` was deliberately left alone.** Zero for all three
  non-administrators, before and after. Requirement 21, criterion 6 keeps periods
  administrator-only and criterion 13 carves out only summaries and pay settings.
  Decision 1 in the migration header covers why the summary read does not need it and
  what the consequence is for the embedded period object in `GET /api/payroll`.

## Row retention (Requirement 23, criterion 3)

Snapshots under `supabase/verification/row-retention/`.

`--verify --label stage-3-pre` reported all seven Time & Attendance tables at delta 0
and `audit_log` at +1. That row is `quote_moved_to_pending_pricing`, written by an
agent at 15:17:15 UTC — Quotes-module activity on a live system, 2.5 minutes after the
snapshot and before the migration ran. It is not a migration write; the migration
contains no DML at all.

Because `--strict` cannot distinguish a concurrent application write from a migration
write, the claim was re-established over a tight window: snapshot, re-apply v1.9.2,
`--verify --strict`. All eight tables delta 0. The re-apply also confirms the migration
is idempotent — still exactly three policies, every assertion satisfied.
